Tuning

Each op ranks its configs per shape in ops::config from the device's measured costs (see Device costs). The ranking is a model, and so is a register count, so neither admits a config. One rule does: the tuner compiles the ranked configs in batches of 64 (config::SHORTLIST), in ranked order and in parallel, until it has the first 64 tiles whose compiled kernels do not spill (a kernel reporting scratch bytes spilled; a backend that reports nothing admits). A tile is admitted at its first clean pipeline (ring depth, unrolling), so an unrolled ring that spills yields to its rolled twin. When every config that compiles spills, the first 64 of those are timed anyway: no op falls to the graph because of spills.

The admitted configs, with their variants (key splits for attention, reduction splits for a starved convolution), are the candidates the tune store measures the first time a device meets a shape. The other pipelines of the leading tiles, those within the contender band of the leader and at most eight (config::TWIN_TILES), are then compiled and timed against the winner, spilling or not, and the fastest is the choice. Attention tunes its softmax forms and its tiles in turn (see the Kernel Library). When nothing compiles (no device to compile on) the first 64 tiles are used untested.

With tuning off nothing is timed: the ranked list's first config that compiles clean runs, found compiling in ranked batches of eight and kept in the store as a first line. The device's cost probes still measure, since the ranking needs them.

When measurement happens​

The op measures where it is called, while the graph is built, never inside a running plan. Each candidate is built as a program, launched on scratch buffers at capacity with every runtime variable bound to its maximum (float operands hold values drawn in [-1, 1), so a card that clocks down under real data is timed as it will run; integer operands hold zeros), and timed:

StepSetting
Warm-upBack-to-back runs until the clock plateaus
RoundsOne round of every candidate, then 3 more of the contenders: those within 1.2× of the first round's best (every candidate, under 16 of them); then 4 more of the two leaders, where more than two contenders ran
Per round5 ms of device time in back-to-back sustain runs (at most 1024 launches, sized by the candidate's last run), then 5 profiled runs, keeping each candidate's minimum
Time of a runThe sum of its kernels' GPU timestamps

The sustain is sized in device time because launches queue asynchronously. A candidate is a list of programs, a kernel plus the merge of its partial results for a split, and its time is the sum of its programs' times. A candidate that fails to build or run is skipped. If nothing measures, the first candidate is used and nothing is stored.

Two guards keep a time honest:

  • One device lock. Every timing in the process, the tuner's and the cost probes' alike, holds one lock: two measurements sharing the device would time each other. Two threads tuning the same key measure it once; the second waits for the first's answer.
  • The stamp guard. A run under half the median of its candidate's runs is a lost stamp and is dropped before the fastest is kept, for the tuner and the probes alike.

The programs field fingerprints the kernels the candidates emit: the lowered program and the LLVM text the backend is handed, so a change to the kernels or the renderer re-measures and a refactor that emits the same kernels does not. What a program emits is remembered by what it is made of, per build of the executable, in emitted/<build>.txt under the store's root (a build unused for a week is removed), so a warm process of the same build lowers nothing and a rebuild lowers each program once. Only a store miss compiles. Writes re-read, merge and atomically replace the file. An unreadable or unwritable store counts as a miss, never an error.

Device costs​

The ranking's costs (atoms::Costs: a shared byte, a streamed byte, a scattered element, a matrix-core step under a full SM, alone on its SIMD and as a warpgroup product, a score's softmax, a pipeline trip over two ring slots unrolled and rolled and over one, the two-slot and one-slot trips of a block alone on its SM, a lane move, a shared round trip, the global latency through two ring slots and, where fills are cp.async, through one, and the L2 rate and size) are measured on the device by probe::costs the first time a process plans for it. Each field is a pair of programs, a probe doing all its baseline does plus a known amount of the primitive. The values go in the same store file as value|probe:<field> lines, so later processes read them back. A probe that fails leaves its field at zero, is warned about, and is named in Planner::unmeasured, which the device gate device_costs_are_physical holds empty: the planner ranks with what it has rather than leaving the device to the graph. Only a device without an SM count has no planner.

Host tests declare costs instead (test::unit::costs(), and gfx1151_costs() pasted from a gfx1151 store). For a card this machine has no device for, Target::offline or Target::card gives the target, and probe::stored reads the costs from a tune store copied from that card; without one, probe::GUESSED stands in and the ranking is a guess. The tk_dump example does both.

The planner log​

RUST_LOG=svod_tk::plan=debug logs the planner's decisions: an op that takes the graph and why, a ranking's first 16 configs with their predicted ns and how many configs it left out, on a store miss what compiling each config showed (verdict, registers, spilled bytes), and the tuned pick. RUST_LOG=svod_tk=debug adds the tuner's rounds.

Switching it off​

HowEffect
SVOD_TK_TUNE=0Candidate timing off; the first ranked config that compiles clean runs (kept as a first line); the cost probes still measure
svod_tk::tune::scoped(false, f)The same for f on this thread (and the workers it compiles on), over the environment; tests use it

tune::TuneStore::at(root) builds a store at another root, or in memory only with None.

Probe tests​

The probe tests are #[ignore] tests that print timings and never assert. They rank with the device's probed costs and time the shortlist the tuner admits, marking the tuner's pick. Run them one at a time on an idle GPU:

SVOD_DEVICE=CUDA cargo test -p svod-tk --release --lib gemm_candidates_probe -- --ignored --nocapture --test-threads=1
